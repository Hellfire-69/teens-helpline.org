## The Core Concept

Maybe it's mean comments on your posts. Maybe a group chat left you out on purpose. Or maybe it's jokes made in the hallway at school. Being picked on or hurt again and again is deeply painful. It often makes you feel ashamed, or like you somehow caused it.

Bullying—whether it happens physically, in words, or online—is about power. The person doing it wants to make you feel small so they can feel big. You are not being "too sensitive" when it hurts. It is meant to hurt.

## What's Actually Going On

When someone is always mean to you, your body stays on high alert. You might always be waiting for the next attack. You might hide away to stay safe.

A few things make bullying feel even harder to deal with:

- **Feeling completely alone.** Bullying works by cutting you off from friends. When others just watch or join in, you might start thinking everyone agrees with the bully.
- **Believing the lies.** When you hear bad things about yourself over and over, you might start wondering if they are true. The insults start to feel like facts.
- **Being scared to speak up.** Many people don't ask for help because they worry the bully will get angry and make things even worse.

None of this is your fault. A bully acts out because they feel insecure, want attention, or don't know how to handle their own feelings. It has nothing to do with what you are worth.

## A Different Way to Look at It

The way you are treated is not about who you are. It is about the bad choices the other person is making. You don't have to change your looks, your hobbies, or your personality to deserve respect and safety.

It also helps to remember you get to pick whose opinions actually matter. Not everyone deserves a vote on your value.

## Key Takeaways

- Bullying is about the other person wanting power, not about your flaws.
- It is perfectly normal to feel scared, angry, or tired when you are targeted.
- You do not have to fix the person who is bullying you.
- Asking for help is how you protect yourself, not a sign of weakness.

## A Small Next Step

You don't have to face the bully or fix everything today. Just pick one small step:

- **Find a safe place.** Think of one place (or person) where you don't have to be on guard. 
- **Keep a quiet record.** Save screenshots, write down dates, and note what was said. You don't have to show anyone right now. Having a record just helps you remember the facts.
- **Tell someone outside the school.** Share what's happening with someone who isn't part of your everyday friend group. They can give you a fresh view on things.

## If It's More Than Stress

If the bullying includes physical threats, severe bullying, or if you feel completely unsafe at school—you must tell an adult who can step in and change the rules.
